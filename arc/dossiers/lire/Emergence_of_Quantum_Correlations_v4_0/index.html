A Relational, Deterministic, and Operator-Free Framework

Filipe Bastos

Abstract

We present a deterministic framework in which the singlet correlation E(a,b)=−cosθabE(a,b) = -\cos\theta_{ab} and the Tsirelson bound 222\sqrt{2} follow from geometric and relational constraints alone, with no Hilbert space, no operator, no collapse and no ontological randomness. A pair shares one relational state λ\lambda on the sphere S2S^2. A first reading takes a sign; the state is replaced by the inverse of that reading on its axis; the second reading is a linear-response threshold. The cosine follows in two lines. The framework has three ceilings, and it states what separates them: the pair read by its sides gives 2, the pair read by its diagonals gives 222\sqrt{2}, and the pair read by the bare sign gives 4. The three follow one another by 2\sqrt{2}, the ratio of the two circles of a square, and the quantum ceiling over the algebraic one equals the inscribed radius over the circumscribed radius. The cosine is exact if and only if the reading has the flat density one half. The model is non-signalling; it is not Bell-local and does not claim to be; the joint table is the same whichever reading is written first. Every number marked as a theorem is recomputed by the companion script.

Keywords: quantum entanglement, Bell inequalities, Tsirelson bound, Popescu-Rohrlich box, deterministic models, geometric projection, relational coherence.

1. Foundational Principles

This work rests on four principles (R).

  • P1. Relational Priority: relations precede objects.
  • P2. Topological Zero Distance: entangled systems share one global relational state.
  • P3. No Ontological Randomness: randomness is epistemic, not fundamental.
  • P4. Structural Closure: coherence is enforced by global constraints, not by dynamics.

2. The Shared Relational State

2.1 (R). Let λ∈S2\lambda \in S^2 be a unit vector representing the relational configuration of a singlet pair. Isotropy is a symmetry constraint: no orientation is privileged. It is the only distribution compatible with the absence of a preferred direction, and it is not an adjustable parameter.

2.2 (T, Archimedes). For λ\lambda isotropic on S2S^2 and any fixed axis aa, the height a⋅λa\cdot\lambda is spread evenly over [−1,1][-1, 1], and the expected magnitude |a⋅λ||a\cdot\lambda| is 1/21/2. With a pencil: wrap the sphere in a cylinder; equal slices of the cylinder cover equal areas of the sphere (Archimedes, On the Sphere and Cylinder); fold the stick at its middle; the balance point of an evenly covered stick is its midpoint. This is the constant k=1/2k = 1/2 of Section 8.

3. Topological Sequential Order

3.1 (R). Measurement events are ordered by a discrete, non-metric, Lorentz-invariant structure TsysT_{sys}. This order sequences relational updates, not spacetime events. There is no preferred frame and no spacetime signal.

3.2 (T, the order is not observable). In the protocol of Section 4 the joint table is P(A,B∣a,b)=14(1−ABcosθab),P(A, B \mid a, b) = \tfrac{1}{4}\,(1 - AB\cos\theta_{ab}), and it is the same whether the reading along aa or the reading along bb is written first. Bob does not depend on Alice any more than Alice depends on Bob: the pair carries both settings together, through their angle.

Theorem 3.3 (T, the order is carried by the state). No order has to be given from outside. Let the face more strongly bound to the state read first: the reading along aa comes first when |λ⋅a|≥|λ⋅b||\lambda\cdot a| \ge |\lambda\cdot b|, the reading along bb otherwise (the degree of binding β=|a⋅λ|\beta = |a\cdot\lambda| of Corpus ARC, Volume 7, statement 3.21). Then each face reads first half of the time, the margins stay at 1/21/2, the joint table is that of 3.2, and |S|=22|S| = 2\sqrt{2}.

Proof. The condition is unchanged when λ\lambda is replaced by −λ-\lambda, which reverses the first sign: given which face reads first, its outcome is ±1\pm 1 with equal weight, and the second reading follows as in Theorem 5.1. ◼\blacksquare

3.4 (R). “Nobody gives the direction. The direction is already there, and the entangled other too.” (the author, 22 November 2026). The written protocol has a first and a second line; the pair has none. Which reading is “first” is a property of the shared state, not a choice and not an event in spacetime.

4. The Protocol

Definition 4.1. Let a,ba, b be unit vectors, and let μ\mu be a second variable, spread evenly over [−1,1][-1, 1], independent of λ\lambda, belonging to the reading.

  1. First reading: A=sgn(λ⋅a)A = \mathrm{sgn}(\lambda\cdot a), A∈{+1,−1}A \in \{+1, -1\}.
  2. Relational update: λ′=−Aa\lambda' = -A\,a.
  3. Second reading: B=sgn(λ′⋅b−μ)B = \mathrm{sgn}(\lambda'\cdot b - \mu), B∈{+1,−1}B \in \{+1, -1\}.

The outcome is deterministic once the complete state (λ,μ)(\lambda, \mu) is given. There is no wavefunction, no operator, no collapse and no free parameter.

4.2 (R). The update is not a signal travelling in spacetime. It is a replacement of coordinate inside the shared state: the pair is one object with two faces, at relational distance zero, and the update activates one face and places the other where the second reading will find it. The pair carries the relation, λ\lambda; the reading carries μ\mu.

5. Emergence of the Quantum Correlation

Theorem 5.1 (T, singlet correlation). Under Definition 4.1, E(a,b)=−cosθabE(a,b) = -\cos\theta_{ab} for all directions.

Proof. By isotropy P(A=+1)=1/2P(A = +1) = 1/2. For fixed λ′\lambda', P(B=+1∣λ′)=P(μ<b⋅λ′)=12(1+b⋅λ′)P(B = +1 \mid \lambda') = P(\mu < b\cdot\lambda') = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + b\cdot\lambda'), since μ\mu is even on [−1,1][-1,1]; hence the expected value of BB given λ′\lambda' is b⋅λ′b\cdot\lambda'. Then the expected value of ABAB given λ\lambda is A(b⋅(−Aa))=−A2(a⋅b)=−cosθabA\,(b\cdot(-A\,a)) = -A^2\,(a\cdot b) = -\cos\theta_{ab}. It does not depend on λ\lambda. ◼\blacksquare

Theorem 5.2 (T, the bound). With S=E(a,b)−E(a,b′)+E(a′,b)+E(a′,b′)S = E(a,b) - E(a,b') + E(a',b) + E(a',b') and the settings a=0°a = 0°, a′=90°a' = 90°, b=45°b = 45°, b′=135°b' = 135°, each of the four terms has magnitude cos45°=122\cos 45° = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{2} and |S|=22|S| = 2\sqrt{2}. Theorem 5.3 (T, non-signalling). P(A∣a)=1/2P(A \mid a) = 1/2 independently of bb, and P(B∣b)=1/2P(B \mid b) = 1/2 independently of aa.

6. The Three Ceilings

Theorem 6.1 (T, the bare sign). If the second reading is the bare sign, B=sgn(λ′⋅b)B = \mathrm{sgn}(\lambda'\cdot b), then AB=−sgn(a⋅b)AB = -\mathrm{sgn}(a\cdot b) for every λ\lambda, so E(a,b)=−sgn(cosθab)E(a,b) = -\mathrm{sgn}(\cos\theta_{ab}), a step function. At the settings of Theorem 5.2 the four correlations are −1,+1,−1,−1-1, +1, -1, -1 and |S|=4|S| = 4: the Popescu-Rohrlich box, the algebraic maximum compatible with no signalling.

Theorem 6.2 (T, the hemisphere). If, after the first reading, the state is taken as spread evenly over the hemisphere of axis −Aa-A\,a and the second reading is its sign, then E(a,b)=−(1−2θab/π)E(a,b) = -(1 - 2\theta_{ab}/\pi), a straight law, and |S|=2|S| = 2: the classical bound.

Reading: the Tsirelson bound is the ceiling of relation without transport. What is instantaneous is the relation; what is bounded by cc is transport.

7. The Half Is Free

Theorem 7.1 (T, the flat density). Keep the protocol, the settings and the update, and change only how μ\mu is spread between its two edges, never favouring a side. If F(c)F(c) is the share of μ\mu below c=λ′⋅bc = \lambda'\cdot b, the correlation is −(2F(c)−1)-(2F(c) - 1). It is the exact cosine at all angles if and only if F(c)=12+12cF(c) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12 c: the density is the constant 1/21/2. Two receipts: a density gathered at the centre, 1−|μ|1 - |\mu|, gives |S|=42−2≈3.66|S| = 4\sqrt{2} - 2 \approx 3.66; a density gathered at the edges, |μ||\mu|, gives |S|=2|S| = 2 at the settings of Theorem 5.2 (over all settings its maximum is 1+2≈2.4141 + \sqrt{2} \approx 2.414, at spacings of 22.5°). Only the flat one gives the cosine, and its maximum over all settings is 222\sqrt{2}.

7.2 (R). “The half is free. It charges nothing when you pass through it; the cost is the price of not passing through it.” (the author, 11 November 2026). Through the half, the reading returns exactly what it receives: if the first face projects the value xx on the second direction, the second reads ++ with chance 12+12x\tfrac12 + \tfrac12 x and the balance of its reading is xx. Any other path distorts. As for the 4 of the bare sign, no measurement gives it: one does not lose what was never there.

Remark 7.3 (T). On the sphere, the part of λ\lambda that the first sign did not read, its height along an axis taken perpendicular to the first axis aa, has by 2.2 exactly this flat density, and it is unlinked to the sign AA. Used as the threshold, it returns the cosine and 222\sqrt{2}. On a circle it does not: the height is no longer evenly spread, and the law falls back to the straight one of Theorem 6.2. What Theorem 7.1 does not say is why the reading carries the flat density; this remark shows where the sphere itself holds it.

8. The Figure: Two Circles and a Square

The diagonal of the square is the diameter of the large circle; its side is the diameter of the small one. The ratio of the radii is 2/2\sqrt{2}/2 and the ratio of the areas is 1/21/2.

Theorem 8.2 (T; R for the identification). Smax=N×k×d=4×12×2=22S_{max} = N \times k \times d = 4 \times \tfrac12 \times \sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2}, and with d=1d = 1, 4×12×1=24 \times \tfrac12 \times 1 = 2. The arithmetic is exact. That N=4N = 4 is the four settings of the square, k=1/2k = 1/2 the projection constant of 2.2, and d=2d = \sqrt{2} the diagonal, is a reading of the known bound, not an independent derivation; in the proof, the 4 comes from the CHSH arrangement.

9. Status of Randomness, and Standard Objections

9.1 (R). The only source of apparent randomness is the dimensional projection S2→{+1,−1}S^2 \to \{+1, -1\}, which discards directional continuity. The Born rule is read as the statistical imprint of that compression.

9.2 (R, determinism without superdeterminism). The state λ\lambda encodes relational constraints between outcomes, not individual outcome values. The measurement angles remain unconstrained by λ\lambda.

9.3 (T, F, locality). The model is non-signalling (Theorem 5.3). Like every model that reproduces the singlet cosine, it is not local in the sense of Bell, and Bell’s theorem (1964) is not contested. In the vocabulary of Jarrett (1984) and Shimony (1986), a protocol written in one order shows parameter dependence without signalling; by 3.2 that order is not observable, and by 3.3 it need not be given. The claim of this paper is the geometric origin of the bound, not a restoration of Bell locality.

9.4 (R, Lorentz invariance). The updates depend only on the joint state and the local directions; the statistics are frame-independent.

9.5 (R, why no operator). The framework is static and relational. Seeking an operator would impose dynamical evolution on a geometric structure.

10. Entanglement as an Inversion Pair

10.1 (R). After the first reading, λ′\lambda' is the inverse of that reading on its axis. This is the structure x↦9−xx \mapsto 9 - x realised physically: the outcome and the updated state form an inversion pair, and neither exists alone. The two outcomes are correlated not because a signal travels, but because they are two readings of one relational state from the two sides of the inversion. Correlation is memory, not causation.

13. Falsifiability

Each of the following refutes the model if violated (P).

  • F1. E(a,b)=−cosθabE(a,b) = -\cos\theta_{ab} exactly, at all angles, for singlet pairs.
  • F2. Smax=22S_{max} = 2\sqrt{2} exactly; any reproducible violation of the Tsirelson bound.
  • F3. P(A∣a)=P(B∣b)=1/2P(A \mid a) = P(B \mid b) = 1/2 for all settings; any observed signalling.
  • F4. k=1/2k = 1/2 for all binary readings on S2S^2.

These agree with standard quantum mechanics: they can refute the model and cannot separate it from quantum mechanics. Two statements of v1.2 are not carried here as tests: the criterion on Δc≠3\Delta c \neq 3, which has no written protocol, and the announcement of deviations from the cosine in “non-standard relational configurations”, which contradicts F1 and has no operational definition (O).

15. Scope and Limits

The model reformulates the measurement of entangled spin-1/2 pairs; extension to several particles is open. It modifies no empirical prediction. An outcome is not stored in an object taken alone: it belongs to the relation, the pair and its reading together. This is principle P1; the received vocabulary calls such a model contextual, and the earlier statement that the model is “not contextual in the sense of Kochen and Specker” is withdrawn. It is not Bell-local (9.3). The decomposition of 8.2 is an exact arithmetic and a reading. Section 12 is arithmetic plus readings. No claim is made that this framework is the only deterministic non-signalling one: Bohm’s theory is deterministic and non-signalling at the level of statistics.

16. Conclusion

Quantum correlations require neither ontological randomness nor collapse. They follow from one shared state on the sphere, one inversion, and one reading of flat density one half. The three known ceilings are three ways of reading the same square: its sides, its diagonals, its bare sign. What quantum mechanics measures at 222\sqrt{2} is the diagonal that the form always had.

Appendix A. Uniqueness of the Relational Update

(T, as stated in v1.2.) The update must satisfy: (1) local outcome consistency, A=sgn(λ⋅a)A = \mathrm{sgn}(\lambda\cdot a); (2) total spin zero for the singlet; (3) minimal information injection, no degree of freedom beyond the outcome. An orthogonal projection λ′⊥a\lambda' \perp a violates (1); a rotation λ′=R(α)λ\lambda' = R(\alpha)\lambda introduces an unconstrained parameter; a scaling violates the unit norm; a stochastic noise violates P3. The only transformation left is λ′=−Aa\lambda' = -A\,a. With Definition 4.1, determinism bears on the complete state (λ,μ)(\lambda, \mu). A continuous λ\lambda cannot be rebuilt from the bit AA and the direction aa.
