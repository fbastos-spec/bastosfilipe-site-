Riemann hypothesis

The Riemann hypothesis: why the line ½

The short answer

The line ½ is the centre of an exchange, s and 1 − s, just as 4.5 is the centre of the pairs in the grid of the nine digits. The corpus does not prove the hypothesis in the language in which it is posed. It shows what the hypothesis is about: the exact number of primes has been counted without a curve and without zeros since 1808, and the zeros are the correction of the curve.

What mathematics establishes

FactIn 1859, in an eight-page memoir, Bernhard Riemann linked the distribution of prime numbers to the zeros of a function, ζ(s), and wrote that it is very probable that all its non-trivial zeros have real part ½. It is one of the seven Millennium Problems of the Clay Institute.

FactThe function satisfies an equation that exchanges s and 1 − s. The fixed point of this exchange is ½.

FactThe rule that gives the exact number of primes below a height is older: Legendre, 1808 (then Meissel, 1870, and Lehmer, 1959). At one hundred million it returns 5,761,455, without a single zero.

FactThe curve of Gauss lands on that same number only on two conditions: remove the squares, cubes and higher powers of the primes, then add the zeros one by one (Riemann's formula, 1859; proved by von Mangoldt, 1895). At one hundred million, without this, it is too high by 753.33: 658.05 for the powers, 95.63 for the zeros, less 0.35 for the origin of the ramp.

FactOn 20 November 2026, Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the hypothesis, written with a machine and certified consistent within its system by Lean: an archive of 17.4 million lines, compiled without failure. The full mathematical exposition remains to be written (status on 25 November 2026).

The reading of the ARC corpus

TheoremThe involution of the grid, φ(x) = 9 − x, brought onto [0, 1], has the fixed point 4.5 / 9 = ½: the same form as Riemann's exchange s ↔ 1 − s (Theorem 9 of Tome I). The corpus itself states that this identity of form, alone, proves no causal link.

TheoremThe exact count is made on two rails, 6k − 1 and 6k + 1, by the activity of the newcomers. Each new prime lays down its multiples starting from its own square, 25 for 5, 49 for 7; the count of one scale is read in the scale below. Kept in two columns, the rule returns the number of primes without visiting a single place. Checked up to one hundred trillion: 3,204,941,750,802.

TheoremThe gap between the curve and the count splits into two lines and one constant, and nothing else: the powers of the primes, the zeros, and 0.352 for the origin. The zeros are what is left of the gap once the powers are removed. This is the theorem of 1895 read in the other direction. “Their zeros are the correction of their tool.”

TheoremProof by projection of scale. The two edges of 6m, 6m − 1 and 6m + 1, have squares whose gap is 24m: one jump of 24 around 6, two around 12, m around 6m. The gap keeps the rank of the turn and gives it back, at the first rank as at the billionth. “If I carry the calculation to that scale, the relation tells me what I will find there.”

Reading“He saw the ½.” Riemann found the centre. He was caught between Euler's endless sum and the curve of Gauss; the question of 1859 questions the instrument, not the numbers. That the curve itself comes from the 9 replaced by 0 is the author's reading, declared as such.

ReadingThe announced proof certifies the construction, the curve and the continuation. It says neither where the primes are, nor why the middle. “They wanted to be certain it would never overflow.”

TheoremA finite face of the hypothesis also exists: Lagarias's inequality on the sum of divisors, verified by the corpus for every integer from 2 to 100,000.

What this page does not say

It does not prove the Riemann hypothesis in the language in which it is posed, and it does not say that the proof of 20 November 2026 is false. It does not claim the counting rule, which is Legendre's. Projection of scale gives the relation at the chosen height; it does not give the impulse of the next step and does not claim to: the word that matters is “if”.
