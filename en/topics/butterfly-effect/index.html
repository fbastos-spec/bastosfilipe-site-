The big questions

Butterfly effect

Is the butterfly effect in nature or in the calculation?

The short answer

In the calculations where it was discovered, the butterfly effect comes from two gestures of the tool: a measurement cut short at the start, and a count of turns thrown away at each step. When the count of turns is kept, the calculation can be traced back exactly. The ARC corpus concludes that this chaos is manufactured by the calculation. What diverged in 1961 was not the sky: it was three retyped decimals.

What is established

FactIn 1961 the meteorologist Edward Lorenz restarts a simulation by retyping a number with three decimals, 0.506, instead of the six the machine was keeping, 0.506127. The two calculations part completely. He publishes in 1963. From this came the image of the butterfly whose wingbeat would change the weather on the other side of the world.

FactThe mechanism is known to mathematicians: a calculation that stretches then folds, and loses at each step a little of what it knew. The corpus does not claim to have discovered it. What it offers is a reading.

Two runners on a track

Imagine two runners on a round track. You take a photo: they are at the same spot. You conclude they are together. But one has run 51 laps and the other 52. The photo shows the position, not the number of laps. That is exactly what a calculation that "folds" does.

TheoremThe calculation, to redo: start from 1/5, and from a number very close to it, 1/5 + 1/256. At each step, double the number and keep only what comes after the decimal point. The gap doubles at each step. At the eighth step the two numbers have the same position, 1/5. Their thrown-away turns, added up, come to 51 for one and 52 for the other. No decimal has been cut: everything is in exact fractions.

The three formulas of chaos

TheoremOne single form: the gap after n steps is the starting gap multiplied n times by the same factor. The corpus distinguishes three engines in it, and each is enough by itself.

TheoremThe truncated seed. Do it yourself. Write one third as 0.333: a third of a thousandth is missing, almost nothing. Multiply by 1000: you get 333, whereas the true result is 333.333… A third is already missing. Multiply again by 1333: 444 is missing. Two exact multiplications, and the starting error has become huge. Over a hundred steps, multiplying by 3 each time, what is missing from one third written 0.3333 becomes a 44-digit number. That is what happened to Lorenz: his seed was truncated, and the calculation grew it.

TheoremThe fold. The measurement is perfect, but at each step the position is kept and the turn is thrown away. These are the two runners.

TheoremThe machine. A computer does both at once. It keeps 52 binary digits; doubling asks for one more at each step; at step 53 nothing of the start is left.

What happens when the turns are kept

TheoremIf the thrown-away turn is written down at each step, the starting point is recovered with no error at all, as far back as you like. In the calculations studied, the confusion disappears as soon as the count of turns is kept.

FactIndependent check: in 2018 Saito and Yamaguchi simulate this same calculation in exact arithmetic, with no rounding at all. It remains chaotic. The chaos of the fold therefore does not come from rounding: it comes from the thrown-away turn.

The ARC reading

Reading"The butterfly effect is not the beat of a wing: it is the fate of a count of turns that the fold throws away." The system was confused with the measurement of the system. What Lorenz saw diverge were numbers handled by a tool that measures, which is legitimate, and then folds while forgetting.

What this page does not claim

It does not say that the weather can be forecast indefinitely. Every measurement is finite, and the corpus holds that limit to be legitimate: that is not what it accuses.

It does not say that Lorenz's equations are wrong. They grow the gap they are given, and they do so exactly. It says that the gap of 1961 was manufactured by the printer, and that with no gap at the start there is nothing to grow.

What would bring this reading down

PredictionProducing a system in which two different starting points become impossible to tell apart although the operation keeps the count of turns. The corpus states that this does not happen, and gives the test to check it.
