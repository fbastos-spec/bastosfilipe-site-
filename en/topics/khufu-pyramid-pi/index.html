Pyramid of Khufu

The Great Pyramid of Khufu and π: what the seked really says

The short answer

The builders of the Great Pyramid did not need to know π. They chose a simple slope, the seked: 5½ palms of run for 7 palms of rise. With that slope, the perimeter of the base is exactly 44/7 times the height, that is twice 22/7, a number very close to 2π. The resemblance to π comes from the slope, not from a secret.

What is established

FactThe Egyptians set the slope of a pyramid by the seked: how far you move back horizontally each time you rise one cubit. A cubit is 7 palms. The seked of the Great Pyramid is 5½ palms.

FactSurveys give a height of about 146.6 metres and a half-base of 115.2 metres. The height divided by the half-base is 1.2726.

The calculation, to redo yourself

TheoremRising 7 while moving back 5.5 is a slope of 7 ÷ 5.5 = 14/11 = 1.2727. That is the measured number, to within one ten-thousandth.

TheoremThe perimeter of the base is 8 half-bases. Divided by the height, that gives 8 × 11/14 = 44/7 = 6.2857. And 2π is 6.2832. That is where the "π pyramid" comes from: from 22/7, the simple fraction the slope produces all by itself.

TheoremThe figure of the ARC corpus, two circles and a square, folded into a pyramid, gives for its part a slope of 1.2720, the square root of the golden ratio. So three numbers sit within a hair of each other: 1.2720 from the figure, 1.2726 from the measurement, 1.2727 from the seked.

What the measurements do not settle

FactThe measured ratio is 0.012% away from 44/7 and 0.053% away from 2π. But Petrie's survey has a margin of error of 0.03% to 0.21%. Both gaps are smaller than that margin: the measurement does not decide.

FactThe corpus had first written "exact agreement" with the golden ratio. It withdrew that wording in September 2026, for the same reason: the gap lies within the margin of error. The withdrawal is kept, dated, in the Register.

The ARC reading

ReadingThe Great Pyramid does not approximate π: it inscribes a closed ratio, 14/11, which can be built with a cord and palms. The "π pyramid" of the books is a consequence of it, not a cause.

ReadingWhat the figure adds is that the same slope comes out of a square held between two circles, with no measurement at all. Whether the builders knew it or not, the corpus does not say.

What this page does not claim

It does not say that the Egyptians knew π or the golden ratio. It does not say that a message is hidden in the pyramid. It says three checkable things: the slope is a simple ratio, that ratio gives 44/7, and the measurements are too loose to choose between the explanations.
