The big questions

Hippasus

What did Hippasus really discover? The diagonal of the square and √2

The short answer

Hippasus showed that the diagonal of a square cannot be written as a fraction of its side. Tradition made a crisis of it: an "irrational" number, a number without reason. The ARC corpus reads something else. The diagonal can be drawn, measured and used perfectly well; what failed was the demand to write it as a ratio of two whole numbers.

What tradition establishes

FactAround 450 BCE, Hippasus of Metapontum, of the school of Pythagoras, studies the diagonal of a square of side 1. He finds that no fraction, however fine, gives its length exactly: the diagonal and the side have no common measure.

FactToday that length is written √2 = 1.41421356…, with decimals that never end and never repeat. This result is proved, and it remains true.

The calculation, to redo with a pencil

TheoremDraw a square of side 1, then build a second square on its diagonal. That large square cuts into four triangles, each of area 1/2. Its area is therefore 2: it is exactly double the first.

TheoremIn other words: the diagonal, multiplied by itself, gives 2. Not roughly, exactly. It is the problem Socrates has a young slave solve in Plato's Meno: to double a square, build it on its diagonal.

TheoremA tail of endless decimals is not always the mark of a mystery. One third is written 0.333… when you count by tens, and simply 0.1 when you count by threes. The tail sometimes comes from the writing, not from the thing.

The ARC reading

ReadingThe Greek word alogos, translated as "irrational", names the failure of a demand: to express the diagonal as a ratio of two whole numbers. It does not name a defect of the diagonal, which is constructed, measurable and works.

ReadingThe diagonal is exact as a relation: its square is 2. It is endless only as a quantity written in decimals. The crisis was not in the diagonal, but in the question put to it.

ReadingThe corpus sees there a mould that repeats later, with Stevin, with Gauss, with Riemann: a demand for foundations is put to something that was working, an impossible object is obtained, and it is given the name of the impossibility.

What this page does not claim

It does not dispute the proof: √2 is not a fraction in any way of counting, by tens, by threes or otherwise. The corpus says so itself. What it discusses is the word "irrational" and the crisis drawn from it, not the theorem.

What would bring this reading down

PredictionThe corpus holds that every crisis of foundations in the history of mathematics comes from a demand for justification put to a calculation that was working. A crisis born from a calculation that stops working by itself, with no such demand, would refute it.
