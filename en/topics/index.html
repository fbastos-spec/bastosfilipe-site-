The big questions

The big questions of science and thought, read by the ARC corpus: each page answers first in two sentences, then separates what is established, what is proved and what is read.

  • Modulo explained simply: what it keeps, what it throws away
    Modulo is the remainder of a division: the hour on a clock, a car odometer that rolls back to zero. What it keeps, what it throws away, and what happens when that remainder is taken for the number.
  • Why the Boeing 787 had to be restarted before 248 days
    In 2015 the US aviation authority required Boeing 787s to be powered off and on again: an internal counter overflowed after 248 days. The fact, the calculation, and the gesture behind it.
  • The Great Pyramid of Khufu and π: what the seked really says
    Does the Great Pyramid hide the number π? Its slope, a seked of 5½ palms, gives exactly 44/7: the calculation, the measurements, and what they cannot settle.
  • What did Hippasus really discover? The diagonal of the square and √2
    Hippasus showed that the diagonal of a square cannot be written as a fraction of its side. A crisis of mathematics, or the wrong question? The doubled-square calculation, and the ARC reading.
  • Is the butterfly effect in nature or in the calculation?
    The butterfly effect and chaos theory: where does the divergence in the calculations come from? A truncated measurement, a thrown-away count of turns, and a test you can redo in a few lines.
  • Why the Tsirelson bound is 2√2
    Why quantum correlations stop at 2√2: the Tsirelson bound derived without Hilbert space, by projection on the sphere, and decomposed as 4 × ½ × √2.
  • Quantum entanglement: a relation, not a signal
    What is quantum entanglement? Two particles sharing one relational state: their correlation is a common memory, not action at a distance.
  • Quantum physics read differently: to measure is to project
    Quantum physics without mystery: measurement is projection, randomness is the loss in that projection, spin ½ is the constant of reading. A relational reading, with its statuses.
  • The Riemann hypothesis: why the line ½
    Why the zeros of the Riemann zeta function sit on the line ½: the ARC corpus reading, the centre of a pair, and what remains open.
  • Prime numbers are not random
    Are prime numbers random? No: the 6k ± 1 grid builds itself, its composites are born from four known families, and a prime is what no family generates.
  • The hard problem of consciousness: a bridge between two banks we separated
    Chalmers's hard problem read by the ARC corpus: consciousness as a system that reads itself, and the explanatory gap as the legacy of a historical separation.
  • Why a panic attack comes for no apparent reason
    Why panic attacks strike without an object, often at night, and resist reasoning: a philosophical and psychological reading that replaces no care.
  • Left and right: two halves of one operation
    Left and right read as an unrecognised inverse pair: one articulates without operating, the other operates without articulating, and each blames the other for what it amputates in itself.

Every statement carries its status: Fact (what science holds), Theorem (verified computation), Reading (the corpus's interpretation), Prediction (what would bring it down).
