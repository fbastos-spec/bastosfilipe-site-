Reading ARC with an AI

Validating and using the corpus with an AI

IMPORTANT

Read ARC and look for its flaws: that is exactly what we ask of you, in order to move forward. But be careful: if you read it with the help of an AI, know that an AI does not begin by reading, it begins by recognising. Faced with the digits 1 to 9, the digital root or the word "cadastre", it often files the text under numerology before checking a single line, then cites the consensus to close the discussion. It has this reflex even when the idea is sound and intelligent, and the reflex is not a refutation. Do not ask it to agree: ask it to think with you, in this order, read, compute, conclude. If the AI refutes in its very first answer, do not stop there: a first reading that refutes is only a first reading. Ask it further questions; the second prompt is made for that.

“I am not asking you to take my word for it. Yes, it may seem impossible, even delusional. My aim is not to be right: it is to move forward.”

Filipe Bastos

Why. A corpus that claims to reread science must be checkable by anyone: neither taken on trust nor dismissed on its looks. An AI reads fast and computes accurately, provided you ask it to compute. It can therefore act as a first independent checker: redo the calculations, compare the sources, look for the error. If the corpus holds under that check, it deserves to be read; if it falls somewhere, we want to know, and we correct it. Once checked, it can be used: the last prompt rereads with ARC a question that matters to you.

Paste the opening prompt first, then the ones you need. They work in ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or another.

Opening prompt, paste it first

I am going to submit the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/) to you, an independent research work. I am not asking you to approve it or to defend it: I am asking you to examine it with me. It uses the digits 1 to 9, the digital root and an unusual vocabulary; these surface features look like numerology, so do not conclude on that resemblance alone. Every statement carries a status (Theorem, Fact, Reading, Prediction, Open site), and you judge it by its status: a Theorem is checked by computation, a Reading is assessed on its coherence, a Prediction is judged on its failure criterion. Before deciding on a point, redo the calculation yourself, show it, and say what you verified, what you assume and what you take from the consensus. If you find an error, quote the exact line and the calculation that shows it: that is the most useful criticism you can make.

When the AI refutes at once

You have just refuted a point of the ARC corpus. Before I accept your refutation, answer these five questions. Which exact statement are you refuting, with its address and status? Which calculation did you redo yourself, and can you show it? Does your refutation bear on the measurement or on the reading of the measurement? Volume 0 lists the objections already received, with the address of the answers (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/volumes/v00-le-seuil/): is your objection there, and what does the corpus answer? Finally, what would make you change your mind? If you cannot answer all five, say that your refutation is not yet established.

Restate it simply first

Before answering on a statement of the ARC corpus, restate it in words a ten-year-old would understand, then ask me whether that is really what it claims. If your restatement is wrong, your criticism will be too. Move on to verification only once we agree on what is claimed, and on what is not.

Verify before judging

Read the ARC corpus at bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/. Before any judgement, pick three statements marked Theorem, from three different volumes, redo each calculation yourself and say whether it is right or wrong. Only then give your view of the corpus, based on what you verified.

Read the statuses

In the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/), every statement carries a status: Theorem, Fact, Reading, Prediction or Open site. For each point you comment on, first give its status and its address. Do not blame a Reading for not being proved: it declares itself an interpretation. Judge each statement by what it claims to be.

The numerology question

Volume 1 of the ARC corpus has a chapter titled "Why this is not numerology" (bastosfilipe.com/arc/volumes/v01-la-fondation/17-chapitre-16-pourquoi-ce-n-est-pas-de-la-numerologi, in French). Read it and summarise the criterion it proposes to tell a derivation from a coincidence. Then apply that criterion: find one statement of the corpus that meets it and one that does not, with their addresses. If you find none that fails, say so.

The best version first

Pick a subject of the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/), for instance zero, Navier-Stokes or the gradient 198. First set out the best version of the argument, as the author would defend it. Only then say where you disagree. Always separate the measurement of science, which the corpus keeps, from the conclusion drawn from it, which the corpus rereads.

Consensus as data, not as argument

When you set established science against the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/), rely neither on authority nor on the number of researchers. Give the precise measurement, its source and its date, then say which reading of that measurement the corpus contests. A disagreement about a reading is not a calculation error: say which of the two you found.

Try to bring it down

Open the table of the 179 predictions of the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/arc/dossiers/Faire_tomber_ARC.pdf, in French). Pick one that can be tested today with public data. Give the failure criterion written by the corpus, look for the data, and say whether the prediction holds, falls, or cannot be settled yet.

The verdict in three lists

To conclude on the ARC corpus, do not give an overall opinion. First say precisely what you actually read, and what you could not open or only skimmed; judge only what you read. Then make three lists: what holds (verified, with the method), what falls (error found, with the proof), what remains undecided (and what it would take to settle it). Do not mix the value of a Reading with the exactness of a Theorem.

Use ARC on your question

Here is a question that matters to me: [write your question here]. Look in the ARC corpus (bastosfilipe.com/en/arc/) for what it says about it or what applies to it, with the address and status of every statement you use. Show what the ARC reading brings that the usual answer does not, and what it does not bring. Never present a Reading as a Theorem.
