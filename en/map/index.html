The map of the corpus

Three gestures, read a hundred times

The ARC corpus has twelve volumes. They are not hundreds of ideas. They are three gestures, found again in arithmetic, physics, history and living things. This page says which ones, and where each reappears.

  • The two circlesWhat closes: the full turn, and the count of turns that must not be thrown away.
  • The squareThe third: what holds the two circles together without their touching.
  • The empty centreWhat stays open: 4.5, the place nobody occupies and through which everything passes.
I

What the tool throws away

We keep the position and throw away the count. Or we cut the tail of the number. Either way, what is missing is written nowhere.

II

The third

What lies between two things counts as one. Two terms that meet always make three.

III

The open form

What does not close is what lets things through: the path, time, the new.

The 7The only digit whose echo runs along both rails without ever touching the axis: 1/7 = 0.142857… It has no place because it is the path. (chapter in French)22/7 and π22/7 is the circle without the link, π the linked circle. The gap between the two is computed exactly.
